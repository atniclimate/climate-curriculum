## 3. The interaction, sketched in words

One screen, two labeled panels that never share a number.

Panel B, the relay (labeled TEACHING MODEL on the panel and on every chart): a strip of eight station tiles in a row (on a phone, a column), named as L3.1 names them. Above it: year type (Cool snowy year, Hot low-water year); fish origin mix (default half Snake River, half mid-Columbia, as in L3.1); and the Round 3 restoration menu from L3.1 (choose up to three; the Ocean tile says "None: discuss mitigation"). Two buttons: "Run one year" and "Run 50 years". One year: 30 fish move tile to tile; each tile shows how many it lost; two bins at the end read "Came Home" and "Fed the People". Fifty years: each year becomes one dot on a dot plot, one row per condition (cool, hot, hot with the chosen restoration). A text line under the plot reads, for example, "50 hot years: came home ranged from A to B; most years near C." What changes when the learner acts: a new condition adds a new row of dots; the earlier rows stay so the spreads can be compared. "Clear" empties the plot. Nothing is saved.

Check values for the build (computed by hand from the seed's invented odds; no simulation run): per fish, cool year 0.223 mid-Columbia and 0.155 Snake River; hot year 0.069 and 0.023. With 30 fish split evenly, about 5 or 6 come home in a cool year and about 1 in a hot year, and roughly one hot year in four sends none home. The page must not round these into claims about real rivers.

Station 6 and the "Fed the People" bin carry the seed's label and dice rule unchanged and no added text. The page states, as L3.1 does, that "Fed the People" is not a loss column. The Nation's own framing of this station: `[NEEDS TRIBAL CONFIRMATION: host Nation]`. Learners never play fishers or a Nation; no tile is voiced by a learner on the page.

Panel A, the river check (labeled SNAPSHOT, with issuer, station, dates, and retrieval date on the panel): a strip chart of one summer of daily water temperature at Bonneville from the case pack. A line marks the temperature threshold; its value and wording come from the VERIFY row, not from this module (D25 is PENDING). The count reads "Days above the line: X of Y days with readings", with a note that EPA's criterion uses a 7-day average of daily maximums, so this count is a classroom count, not a test of the criterion. The L3.1 oxygen table sits beside it as a small reference table. Optional: one other relative's physical line (for example bull trout) appears only once its VERIFY row is CHECKED, with its English common name only.

## 4. Hand twin

- Paper relay (L3.1 Part B as written, 40 min): stations, dice, salmon cards, two bins. One rule added: no learner voices Station 6; its card is read as written.
- Wall dot plot (2 min per round, then across days): chart paper with two rows, cool and hot. After each round the class places one sticky dot for its "Came Home" count. Other periods and sections add their dots on following days; by the end of the week the paper shows a spread. This is the slow, honest version of "Run 50 years".
- Dice tally sheet (8 min, pairs): one die, one printed sheet with the eight stations in a row. A pair runs five fish through a cool year and five through a hot year, ticking each roll. The class pools the tallies on the wall plot (2 min). Works with no device and no internet.
- Part A paper (10 min): the case-pack temperatures printed as a dated table and a blank graph; learners plot and count with a highlighter; a missing day is a gap marked "no reading".

## 7. Access

- Keyboard: Tab through year type (radio group), origin mix, restoration checkboxes (a fourth choice is refused with a spoken message), Run one year, Run 50 years, Results table, Clear, then Panel A. Visible focus on every control; Enter and Space activate.
- Screen reader: each tile has a label ("Station 5, Bonneville: lost 3 this year"). After a run, one polite live message gives the summary line. The dot plot has a text alternative and a table toggle. Both panel labels (TEACHING MODEL, SNAPSHOT) are text, not color.
- Reduced motion: no fish movement; counts and dots appear at once.
- Chromebook: 50 years of 30 fish is about 13,500 dice rolls, trivial at 4x throttle. The page is one HTML file with inline SVG and a small inline snapshot (about 90 values); weight is mostly the existing subset fonts (estimate, not measured).
- 390px: the station strip becomes a column of eight tiles; controls stack above; the dot plot and Panel A go full width below. Targets at least 44px.
- No names, scores, or uploads; session memory only; no network calls.

## 8. Minutes

- Workshop excerpt (3 to 5 min, after s10): run one cool year and one hot year (1 min); "Was one hot year enough to know?" (1 min); run 50 of each and read the spread (1 to 2 min); point to the TEACHING MODEL label and say the odds are invented (30 s).
- Full lesson: L3.1 stays two 50-minute periods. Part A step 4 uses Panel A (10 min). Part B step 5 adds the 50-year runs (8 to 10 min, inside the existing tally block, which may run 4 minutes over; the teacher trims Round 3 or the graph instead).
