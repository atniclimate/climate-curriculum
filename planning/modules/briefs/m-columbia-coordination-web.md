# m-columbia-coordination-web: Who Touches the Decision?

Cluster L. Status: concept brief, planning only (no build before D27).
Module author: Patrick A. Freeland, with ATNI Climate. Licensing to be set later.
Seeds: P4, L4.1, T-M4, T-SA5, s20. Written from SEEDS.md as of commit 835c6a1.

The module maps who touches one decision on a river: by role and institution,
never by a person's name, and with no learner holding, voicing, or acting as a
Nation, council, Elder, or knowledge holder (D22, PENDING, applies to every
module). It differs from cluster K (m-columbia-hazard-chain) in one line: K
follows a physical stress down the river; L follows a decision through the
people and institutions who hold authority over it, and the doors between them.

## 3. The interaction, sketched in words

One screen, labeled "TEACHING MODEL: an invented creek and an invented
decision. Roles, not people."

- Center card: the decision, invented on purpose: "Replace a county road
  culvert that blocks a salmon creek." Around it, three rings like s20:
  water and relatives (River, Salmon, Groundwater) inside; people and
  institutions outside (County public works, State transportation agency,
  Irrigation district, Federal grant program, Federal fish agency, InterTribal
  fisheries commission, School, Families).
- Two anchored nodes sit apart, drawn as open circles with a fixed caption:
  "Tribal Nation government: speaks only for itself, through its own
  representatives and public record" and "Elder or knowledge holder: not a
  role anyone in this room plays." Learners draw strands to them; they cannot
  open, rename, type into, or answer for them.
- The learner picks two nodes, then a strand kind (ecological, economic,
  legal, cultural). A cultural strand to an anchored node reads only "a
  relationship the Nation describes" and holds no text.
- A row of five door cards (consultation, comment, testimony, co-management,
  courts) sits under the web. Choosing a door shows a short fixed card: who
  opens it, who must answer, what gets recorded, and the source with the date
  checked (section 9). A learner places a door on a strand.
- One switch: "Skip the consultation door." The consultation strand goes
  dashed, the legal strands it feeds thicken, and a sentence appears: "Nobody
  asked. Count the strands that changed." The page shows the count, not a
  story. Switching off restores the web.
- Projector view shows counts only (strands by kind; doors placed; the most
  connected role by its role label). Any learner-added node is a role or
  office picked from a list; text stays in session memory and clears on
  reload.
- Three scales: Frog's Eye is one strand you hold; Bird's Eye is the whole web
  on this creek; Moon's Eye is a door that reaches outside the basin (a
  federal rule, or an international river agreement), named on a card only.

## 4. Hand twin

The yarn web is the primary form; the page is a later companion.

- Anchored cards (the D22 proposal, not a ruling): any card naming a Tribal
  government, a Tribal person, or a Tribal institution is not worn. On L4.1's
  list that is Tribal Nation government, Elder or knowledge holder, Tribal
  fisher, CRITFC scientist, and First Nation in the Canadian upper Columbia
  (question 2). Each is printed large and taped to an empty chair or post
  inside the circle. Learners loop yarn around the chair; no one stands in
  for it, tugs for it, or speaks for it.
- The consultation event: the learner wearing "Federal agency" reads the
  printed card ("An agency decides without consulting the Tribe") and tugs.
  The strand to the Nation's chair goes slack, because no one asked. Each
  learner who feels the tug says how, as their own role. The teacher asks
  L4.1's question: why did a social strand move the whole web?
- Door cards (5 minutes inside the 50): five half-sheets laid on the floor.
  Pairs lay one door on a strand and say who must answer there; 9-12 read the
  source line on the back.
- More than one way in and out: feel the tug, see the strand, hear the card
  read; respond by speaking, pointing, writing on the sketch, or tying a
  colored tag (ecological, economic, legal, cultural tags also carry a letter
  E, $, L, C, so color is never the only cue). Learners who cannot stand hold
  a strand from a seat; the web is built at seated height if needed.
- No device, no internet, nothing collected. Sketches go home.

## 6. Place

- Neutral form: an invented creek with a county road, a highway, and an
  irrigation district. No real place name, no reservation, no usual and
  accustomed area, no Nation named.
- Nation overlay: a Nation could add its own published consultation protocol
  or its own list of doors, quoted from its record, and say what is never on
  the table `[NEEDS TRIBAL CONFIRMATION: host Nation]`. That is C2: the
  module would present that Nation's governance, which only it can authorize.
  An overlay never adds a person, a Tribe-specific place, or a cultural
  relationship to the web.
- Consent level: C0 for the neutral form. Nothing in it is Tribe-specific;
  the anchored nodes are positions in a system, empty by design.
- Teacher note: if a learner from a Nation is in the room, never ask them to
  say what their Nation would do or to take an anchored card.

  strand. Visible focus ring; 44px targets.
- Screen reader: every node is a button with its role label; anchored nodes
  read their full caption. A strand table (From, To, Kind, Door) mirrors the
  web and is the default view under 600px. A polite live region announces
  "Strand added: County public works to River, ecological. 7 strands."

  thick, and the table lists them.
