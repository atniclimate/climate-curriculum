## 3. The interaction, sketched in words

One screen, two panels that never share an axis, because one is a TEACHING MODEL and the other is a SNAPSHOT. The record panel comes first; the screen closes on the accounts panel, where the learner acts.

**Panel 1, Read the air (SNAPSHOT).** The monthly CO2 line from the frozen NOAA file, March 1958 to the pack's last month, with the annual means as a second line the learner can switch on. A dashed reference line at the pre-industrial value, labeled with its own source (not from this file). Controls: "Last three years" and "Whole record" zoom buttons; arrow keys step month by month; a readout gives month, year, ppm, and the row's status. In the three-year zoom the learner marks each year's high and low month. The page then shows the months the file holds; it does not claim the high is always May (section 9, J5). Rows the file says were filled in by NOAA show as hollow points: "filled in by NOAA, not measured." The Scripps months (03/1958 to 04/1974) sit in a band: "this file does not say which of these months were measured." The months from 12/2022 to early 07/2023 sit in a band: "measured at Maunakea, about 21 miles north, after the eruption." A "Print this chart" button prints the chart with title, source, and capture date stamped on it.

**Panel 2, Run the accounts (TEACHING MODEL).** Four accounts, drawn as four jars of beans with a number under each: air, ocean, land and living things, fossil carbon. The natural flows (plants take in and give back, the ocean takes in and gives back) balance, so with nothing else on, the air's account holds steady. Three levers, one per scale:

- **Frog's Eye: plant a tree.** One bean a year moves from air to land, after a few model years of growing; it stays in the land account while the tree and soil hold it.
- **Bird's Eye: keep forests and wetlands holding carbon.** Turns off a small land-to-air flow.
- **Moon's Eye: the fossil scoop.** Beans per year moved from fossil carbon to air (0, 1, 2, or 4).

Before running, the learner sets a prediction mark on the air jar ("After 50 years, the air's account will be about here"). Then "Run 1 year," "Run 10 years," or "Run 50 years." The jars and numbers change; a small table logs each year. The land and ocean take back part of any extra beans, at a share the model invents and labels. With the scoop on, the air's account climbs even with the tree planted; with the scoop at 0, the tree pulls it down slowly. Every number in this panel is invented and says so in the label.

**The close (climate emotions, Grades 6-12 lines 27 to 36: "No lesson ends on a projection").** The last thing on screen is the accounts panel set by the learner, with the CCIC reflection beneath it: "Carbon moves in circles. Where do you sit in that circle, and what part of it is yours to change?" (line 781). Nothing ends on the rising curve. Wording for both panels goes to Patrick's voice pass; the CCIC instructor note (line 785) holds: blame and individual guilt stay out of the center.

## 4. Hand twin

- **Bean accounts (15 minutes, the CCIC lab as written, line 775):** four labeled cups or bowls, about 200 dried beans or tokens, an account table on paper. Four account keepers move beans each "year" on the teacher's call; the class runs five years without fossil beans, then five with two fossil beans a year into the air cup. A fifth learner plants a "tree" by moving one bean a year from air to land from year 3.
- **Paper curve (15 minutes, no device, no internet):** a printed sheet from the case pack with the annual means pre-plotted and a table of the last 36 monthly values. Pairs plot the 36 points by hand and circle each high and low; a yarn line along the annual means shows the trend.
- **More than one way in:** hands (beans, plotting), eyes (jars and graph), voice (account keepers call their counts aloud; a reader role for a learner who should not handle small objects), and writing (the account table and the three sentences). A raised-line copy of the three-year zigzag (glue line on card stock, about 10 minutes of teacher prep) serves a learner who reads by touch. Where young children may be in the room (CCIC Family-Friendly Classroom, line 914), paper squares replace beans.

## 7. Access

- **Keyboard path:** zoom buttons, then the curve (arrow keys step months, Home and End jump to the ends, Enter marks a high or low), then the annual-means switch, then Print; then the three levers, the prediction slider, and the three Run buttons. Visible focus on every control; targets at least 44px.
- **Screen-reader labels:** the curve has a text summary and a "Show as table" switch (one row per year: high month, low month, annual mean, status notes). After each run, a polite live region reads the four accounts ("Year 10: air 112, ocean 96, land 104, fossil 380; invented numbers"). Hollow points and bands carry their words, not color alone.
- **Reduced motion:** beans do not animate; jars and numbers update in one step. Same when prefers-reduced-motion is set or the learner turns animation off.
- **Chromebook load:** both CSVs together are about 42 KB; one SVG polyline of about 820 points and four jars. Expected light at 4x CPU throttle; not measured.
- **390px wide:** panels stack, record panel above, accounts below; the curve opens on the three-year zoom; the account table scrolls on its own.
- **Storage:** prediction marks and high and low marks live in session memory only (D23 PENDING; no default is assumed). No analytics, no network calls; the page runs from a downloaded folder.
