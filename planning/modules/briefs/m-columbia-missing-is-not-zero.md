# m-columbia-missing-is-not-zero: Missing Is Not Zero

Cluster A. Status: concept brief, planning only (no build before D27).
Module author: Patrick A. Freeland, with ATNI Climate. Licensing to be set later.
Seeds: P2, L3.3, L4.3, C-A7, T-SA2, T-SA4, s08, s16, s17. Written from SEEDS.md as of commit 6784076.

This is the D27 default pilot (D27 is PENDING): a paper-first lesson authored by
hand before any module skill or page engine exists. The paper set is the
product. The one-screen page in section 3 is a later companion, built only
after the paper version is rehearsed. Everything in it is invented on purpose
(TEACHING MODEL): no real place, no real Nation, no real case.

## 1. Seeds served and the socket slides

- P2 (How Do You Know What You Know?): a blank is a question about the
  record, not an answer. This is "read your sources" applied to data.
- L3.3 (DDM field study): the rule learners need before they open a live map.
  A tool's "unavailable" or "no data" is not "nothing here". See the L3.3
  defect noted in section 9 (V-A3).
- L4.3 (held under D22): offers only a neutral siting form on an invented
  watershed grid. No real case, place, or Nation; no role play of any
  government. This brief does not draft, revise, or replace L4.3; that waits
  on Patrick's D22 ruling.
- C-A7 (Framing Worksheet): "a full, honest worksheet with gaps named is
  better than a polished one" (CCIC 200 grading note) becomes a practiced
  move: an empty cell reads "unknown" plus how you would find out.
- T-SA2 (exposure table, Template H) and T-SA4 Part C (land note): the
  difference between "not found", "unknown", and "may be held internally",
  practiced on an invented cell before a real one.
- Socket slides: s08-three-eyes (each eye has a blank it cannot see past),
  s16-job-sort (its caption says some solutions harm "in the wrong place, or
  without consent"; the grid rehearses asking before siting), and
  s17-knowing-to-doing (step 1 Observe and the "Sovereignty first" callout:
  data about a Nation belongs to that Nation).
- Cross-reference: library/_synthesis/place-based-modules.md section 2.2,
  card PM-04 (private proposal, 10/14/2026; locator only, nothing copied).
  This brief differs: its core is a two-week gauge record, and its grid's grey
  cells have an unstated reason, so learners cannot assume a grey cell holds
  a Nation's data.

## 3. The interaction, sketched in words

One screen, later companion, labeled "TEACHING MODEL: invented school rain
gauge, invented creek, invented numbers."

- Top strip: fourteen day boxes (Week 1 and Week 2). Numbers show in mm.
  Blank days show a hatched grey box with the word "unknown" and the reason
  if one is given ("gauge not read"). A true 0 shows as "0 mm" in plain type.
- Beside each week: its total. A week with a blank reads "at least 9 mm", not
  "9 mm".
- One switch: "Count blanks as zero." When on, the hatched boxes turn into 0,
  the totals drop the "at least", and a sentence appears: "Week 1 now looks
  drier. Did it rain less, or did no one read the gauge?" Switching off
  restores the honest view. Nothing animates.
- Below: the 5 by 5 invented watershed grid (columns A to E, rows 1 to 5),
  each cell carrying symbols for slope, road reach, and stream. Six cells sit
  under a grey "not shown on this map" layer. The learner picks two side by
  side cells for a small project. Each grey cell picked opens two short text
  boxes: "Who must be asked (a kind of office or government, never a real
  name)" and "How would you find out?" No score, no green light, no "best
  site." Text stays in session memory only and clears on reload.

## 4. Hand twin

The primary product. One letter page of cards, one page of grid and form, one
half-page teacher card. Black and white, photocopy-safe.

- Gauge cards (12 minutes, pairs): fourteen cards. Week 1: Mon 4, Tue 0,
  Wed 2, Thu 0, Fri 3, Sat unknown, Sun unknown. Week 2: Mon 1, Tue 0, Wed 5,
  Thu 2, Fri 0, Sat 3, Sun 2 (mm; invented). Unknown cards are hatched, say
  "unknown: gauge not read", and have a hole-punched corner so they can be
  found by touch. Pairs lay out both weeks, add each week, and answer "Which
  week was drier?" Then they move the unknown cards to their own pile and
  answer again. Week 1 counted is 9, Week 2 is 13; with the blanks honest,
  Week 1 is "at least 9" and the question has no answer yet.
- Kinds of blank (3 minutes, whole class): four reason cards read aloud and
  held up: not read; broken; never measured here; held by its owner and not
  shared. The teacher says plainly that the last one is the owner's choice,
  and the owner decides who sees it. Each pair lays one reason card on its
  unknown pile, or the card "we cannot tell which", and says why (evidence).
- Siting grid and form (18 minutes, groups of three or four): the printed
  5 by 5 grid with six hatched cells. Rule printed at the top: "Grey is not
  green. A blank on a map means ask, not go. No one on your team speaks for
  a government; you are planners writing questions." The form has one row
  per cell used: what the map shows, what it does not show, who must be
  asked (a kind, not a name), how you would find out. Last line: "Our pick,
  and the one unknown we need answered first."
- More than one way in: cards can be placed, pointed to, or read aloud; the
  form can be written, drawn, or dictated to a partner; the grid has letters
  and numbers on every edge for learners who cannot use color or pattern.
- 6-8: two hatched cells, a sentence frame on the form. 9-12: all six, plus
  "If no one emptied the gauge over the weekend, what might Monday's number
  hold?"
- No internet, no device, nothing collected. Forms go home with learners.

## 6. Place

- Neutral form: an invented creek and an invented school. No place name, no
  real geography, no Nation, no map of any real watershed. The id carries
  "columbia" as the family name only (see question 8); the page never says
  the grid is on the Columbia.
- Nation overlay: none proposed. This module stays synthetic by design. A
  Nation could, if it chose, add its own words about how outsiders should
  ask before using its homelands, replacing the generic "who must be asked"
  prompt `[NEEDS TRIBAL CONFIRMATION: host Nation]`. That would be C2 at
  least, C3 if it touched place names or First Foods, and only on that
  Nation's initiative.
- L4.3's real case stays out of this module and out of this brief (D22).
- Consent level: C0. Nothing here is Tribe-specific, place-specific, or a
  Nation's data. Teacher card says: do not let learners write a real Nation,
  real site, or real place name onto the grid; if a learner from a Nation is
  in the room, never ask them to say what their Nation would do.

## 7. Access

- Keyboard: Tab order runs day boxes, the switch, then the grid row by row;
  arrow keys move within the grid; Space or Enter picks a cell; visible
  focus ring; 44px targets.
- Screen reader: each day box reads, for example, "Week 1, Saturday: unknown,
  gauge not read"; totals read "Week 1 total: at least 9 millimeters"; each
  cell reads "B3: steep slope, road reaches, grey: not shown on this map";
  the switch result sentence sits in a polite live region.
- Reduced motion: there is no motion; the switch changes text and pattern
  only.
- Chromebook load: one self-contained HTML file with inline SVG patterns and
  no photos, estimated well under 100 KB (not measured); 4x CPU throttle test
  required at build.
- 390px: the day strip wraps to two rows of seven; the grid stays 5 by 5 at
  about 64px per cell; the form fields stack below the grid.
- Offline: no network calls; runs from a downloaded folder. Session memory
  only, consistent with the D23 default (PENDING).

## 8. Minutes

- Workshop excerpt (3 to 5 minutes): show both weeks (projector or two
  printed strips), ask "Which week was drier?", take a show of hands, flip
  the two unknown cards, ask again, state the rule. Hand out the paper set.
- Full lesson (50 minutes): frame with the s08 question, "What can each eye
  not see?" (0 to 5); gauge cards, Frog's Eye (5 to 17); kinds of blank (17
  to 20); siting grid and form, Bird's Eye (20 to 38); each group reads its
  "first unknown" line, or posts its form for the class to read, while the
  teacher lists the kinds of "who must be asked" on the board (38 to 45);
  Moon's Eye question, "Somewhere on the planet there is no gauge at all;
  does that place have no rain?", then the exit ticket, written, drawn, or
  said to the teacher (45 to 50).
- CCIC 200: 15 minutes inside Week 7 Application. TCR 201: 10 minutes at the
  start of Week 8 Station 3.
- Not yet rehearsed. D27's default says rehearse before deciding on a module
  skill; times above are design estimates, not tested.
