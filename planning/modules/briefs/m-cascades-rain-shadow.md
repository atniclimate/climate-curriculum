## 3. The interaction, sketched in words

One screen. Across the top is a schematic cross section, Pacific to Columbia
Plateau. It is drawn by the module itself and labeled TEACHING MODEL: "Shape
simplified; not to scale." Two pins sit at the same height on opposite sides
of the crest: a west station in a Columbia tributary valley and an east
station on the dry side. Under each pin is a station card that gives the
issuer, the id, and the elevation.

1. Predict. A four-choice group asks, "Which side gets more rain and snow in a
   year?": about the same; about twice as much on the west; about five times;
   seven times or more. A second control lets the learner skip predicting.
   Nothing is scored and nothing is stored.
2. Reveal. Twelve monthly bars rise under each pin, the 1991 to 2020 normals,
   labeled SNAPSHOT with the issuer, version, and capture date. A sentence
   below gives both yearly totals and how many times larger the west total
   is. The learner's prediction appears beside it without judgment.
3. Step the months. Previous and Next buttons (and the arrow keys) move a
   highlight one month at a time. The sentence updates, for example: "In this
   month the west station's normal is larger than the east station's whole
   year." That fact holds for one month; see section 9.
4. One storm (a toggle, for 9-12 and CCIC 200). The view switches to about ten
   days of daily totals at both stations, from one dated storm, plus two
   river gauges shown as flow per square mile. It is labeled SNAPSHOT. An
   optional satellite water-vapor still for the storm date sits beside it,
   also SNAPSHOT.

What changes when they act: the bars, the highlight, the readout sentence,
and the labels. Nothing leaves the page.

## 4. Hand twin

"Rain ribbons," 10 to 12 minutes, no device.
- A strip of masking tape on the floor or a table is the crest. Two paper
  station cards (name, id, elevation, 12 monthly normals) sit on either side.
- Groups cut paper strips at 1 cm per inch of normal precipitation, one per
  month, and lay them as a bar chart beside each card. The west year runs
  about 58 cm and the east year about 8 cm, so the difference shows in the
  hand before any number is read aloud.
- Movement version: learners walk the path the air takes. They rise and lift
  their arms going up the west slope (cooling, dropping "rain" strips), and
  lower their arms coming down the east side (warming, drying). Seated
  learners can trace it on the printed cross section with a finger.
- One month card is left blank and printed "no reading: unknown." Groups say
  what they would do with it. The answer is not "zero."
- Printable from the case pack. It needs no internet and no projector.

The two rivers drain basins of very different sizes, so the raw flows cannot
be compared directly. The page shows flow per square mile, and the CCIC 200
band does that division itself.

Blank or missing values: a missing month or day is drawn as a dashed hollow
outline with the visible text "no reading: unknown," and screen readers hear
the same words. A trace of rain is labeled "trace." Neither is ever drawn or
counted as zero. The case-pack converter must turn the issuer's
missing-value code into "unknown," never 0. That code is in the GHCN-Daily
readme, not opened (section 9).

## 6. Place

- Neutral form (what ships): public geography (the Cascade crest, the Cowlitz
  and Yakima valleys, town names as issuers spell them in station names) and
  public agency records. It has no Nation content, place names in a language,
  First Foods, or stories.

  see Question 5.

## 7. Access

- Keyboard path: prediction group (radio buttons, arrow keys inside, Tab out),
  then Skip, then Previous month and Next month buttons, then the "Year / One
  storm" toggle (aria-pressed), then "Show as a table." Focus is always
  visible. Every target is at least 44px.
- Screen readers: the cross section has a title and a description, like s04.
  Every bar has a text equivalent. A polite live region reads the month
  sentence. A real data table with captions and units carries every value
  shown, including "unknown" and "trace."
- More than one way to perceive: west and east differ by position, label,
  and fill pattern (hatched versus solid), never by color alone. Every number
  shown as a bar is also written as text.
- Reduced motion: with prefers-reduced-motion, the bars appear at full
  height and the highlight moves without sliding.
- Chromebook load: two small CSV-derived JSON blocks and inline SVG, about 40
  to 80 KB before fonts (estimate, not measured). Only CSS transitions, no
  animation loop. Test at 4x CPU throttle.
- 390px: the cross section stacks above the charts. Each station's 12 months
  become a horizontal bar list, west first. The table view replaces the
  charts on request.
- Offline: one self-contained page plus the case-pack files. It makes no
  network calls and works from a downloaded folder.
- Student input: the prediction lives in session memory only and is not
  saved. This matches the D23 default, which is PENDING.
