Seeds: P2, L3.3, C-W6, C-A5, T-M2, T-M3, T-SA1, T-SA2, s08, s10, s17. Written from SEEDS.md as of commit 9c8d64f.

docs/m-columbia-now-and-ahead.html. Learner-facing wording below is a
placeholder for Patrick's voice pass, not final copy.

The one rule this module teaches: what was measured (SNAPSHOT) and what might
come (a projection, with its scenario, period, baseline, and source named)
sit side by side and are never joined into one line, one color, one column,
or one table. A projection is not a prediction; it is an "if" statement.

## 3. The interaction, sketched in words

One screen, two panels with a fixed gap between them.

- Left panel, OBSERVED. Label bar: "SNAPSHOT: NOAA NCEI Climate at a Glance, [division or county by agency name], [variable], [first year] to [last full year], captured [MM/DD/YYYY]." One solid dot per year. A year with no value sits on Brief A's "No reading recorded" rail, never at zero, never joined across.
- Right panel, PROJECTED. Label bar: "PROJECTION, not a prediction: [source and version], [n] models, change from [baseline years], captured [MM/DD/YYYY]." For each scenario and period, a hatched bar from the lowest to the highest model with a tick at the middle. Each bar carries four printed tags: scenario, period, baseline, source. A bar with any tag missing does not draw.
- The gap between panels is a labeled band: "Measured on the left. 'If the world follows this pathway' on the right." No line, color, or shape crosses it. Observed marks and projected bars never share a fill, a pattern, or a legend entry.
- Control 1, "Compared to what?": switches the observed panel from raw values to change from the projection's own baseline years. The sentence under it names both baselines. If the record does not cover those years, the switch is absent and says why.
- Control 2, "Add the next period": the right panel starts with mid-century bars for both scenarios side by side, then adds late century. Both scenarios always show together; there is no toggle that hides one. A caption under the bars, written once by Patrick, says what separates them [PERSONAL VOICE: Patrick writes this].
- Control 3, "One box, two places" (collapsed by default; TCR 201 and 9-12): an invented valley-to-ridge profile, labeled TEACHING MODEL, under one square grid cell. The cell reports one averaged number; the valley and the ridge each differ from it. No real place, no boundary.
- Table view: two separate tables, one per panel. The rule holds in the table too.

What changes when the learner acts: the observed panel relabels; bars are
added; sentences under each panel update and are announced. Nothing merges.

## 4. Hand twin

Built from the same case pack, so paper and page agree. Black and white.

- Two sheets, never taped together (15 min): Sheet 1 is the observed dot strip; Sheet 2 is the range bars. A strip of tape on the desk is "the gap". Pairs answer three questions written on the gap strip: which sheet was measured, what each bar is "compared to", where the two scenarios overlap and where they separate.
- Tag clips (5 min): four paper tags (scenario, period, baseline, source) clip onto each bar. An untagged bar is turned face down: "not readable yet."
- Movement version (10 min): two sides of the room with a taped gap. Two students per bar hold a length of yarn from low to high for each scenario. At mid-century the two yarns overlap; at late century they step apart. Students on the observed side hold year cards. No one crosses the gap.
- Statement sort (6-8, 8 min): six cards ("Summers here were warmer in the last 20 years than in the 1950s"; "Late-century summers will be hotter"; others) sorted into measured, what if, or can't tell; for "can't tell", the learner names the missing tag. Card wording is a placeholder for the voice pass; any card stated as measured must be checked against the case pack before it prints.
- Spot the blur (CCIC 200 Week 6 step 4; 5 min): one invented chart, labeled "TEACHING MODEL: a chart that breaks the rule; invented numbers", where one line runs from past into future in one color. Learners circle what it hides: which future, compared to what, and the spread. Paper only (question 6).
- One box, two places (TCR 201, 9-12; 5 min): a printed profile and a transparent square; learners say or write what the square's single number hides.
- Ways in and out: look, listen (values read aloud), hold yarn, or stand; answer by pointing, saying, writing, or drawing. Scenarios differ by pattern and printed label, never by color alone. No internet, no device, nothing collected.

## 7. Access

- Keyboard: Tab order is the two label bars, "Compared to what?", "Add the next period", "One box, two places", "Show as tables". Arrow keys move year to year in the observed panel and bar to bar in the projected panel; Home and End jump; visible focus; 44px targets.
- Screen reader: an observed mark reads "[year]: [value] °F, measured"; a bar reads "[scenario], [period], change from [baseline]: from [low] to [high], middle [mid], [n] models; projection, not a prediction". Panel sentences sit in a polite live region. The gap band has a heading so the two panels are announced as separate regions.
- Not color alone: observed is solid dots; projected is hatched bars; the two scenarios use different hatch angles and printed names. Existing site colors only; no new palette.
- Reduced motion: no animation; bars appear in place.
- Chromebook load (estimate, not measured): one inline SVG, about 130 dots and 8 bars, case pack under 50 KB inlined; no animation loop. Confirm at 4x CPU throttle at build.
- 390px: panels stack, observed first, with the gap as a labeled full-width divider; bars turn horizontal.
- Offline and storage: no network calls; runs from a downloaded folder. The optional sentence box keeps text in session memory only, matching the D23 default (PENDING). No analytics.
