## 3. The interaction, sketched in words

One screen, three parts.

1. A deck of message cards. Each card face shows the message, a "reached you by"
   tag (a feed, a poster, a long film, a public meeting, a headline, a report), and a
   source line: issuer and date, or "written for this lesson" for invented cards.
2. A board with two axes. Across: "Fills it all in for you" to "Asks you to fill in
   the gaps" (McLuhan's hot and cool in plain words; DRAFT wording; see V12 and
   question 1). Up and down: Frog's Eye (made here, close to this river), Bird's Eye
   (made in this region), Moon's Eye (made far away, at planet scale). Beside the board,
   a "Can't tell" tray: a real place to put a card, not a failure.
3. The scaffold bar, kept from s09: Notice, Ask, Check.

What the learner does: selects a card, chooses a spot (a click on the board, or two
five-step choices, one per axis), then picks a reason (short chips such as "it tells
me what to feel," "it gives me numbers I can look up," "I don't know who made it,"
plus an optional typed sentence). The reason is required before the card settles.

What changes: the settled card opens its Notice, Ask, Check panel. Notice asks what
the learner felt first (chips: calm, worried, angry, hopeful, tired, nothing, other).
Ask and Check show the card's teacher note under the heading "One way to see it,"
never "answer." No position is marked right or wrong; no count or score appears. A
one-line note on the Moon's Eye row says distance is not a trust score: a planet-scale
record and a voice from here can both be true. The learner may star one card, "I felt
this before I thought about it." Reset clears everything.

The "who speaks for a place" thread lives in Ask ("Who made this, and do they live
with what it describes?"), asked of each message. Learners judge their own reactions
and the message's channel; they never rate a Nation's voice, never write words for a
place's people, and never stand in for anyone. A fixed panel at the board's edge,
outside the deck and not sortable, marks where a host Nation's own published words
could appear: `[NEEDS TRIBAL CONFIRMATION: host Nation]`. In the neutral form it
stays empty (question 6).

Hype and talking points enter as cards (a looping slogan, a scroll-bait post, both
written for this lesson), not as stated facts about feeds (row P-9).

Storage: session memory only. Nothing saved by default, no network call, nothing
leaves the page. If D23's pattern is later ruled for s23, this module may follow it.

## 4. Hand twin

- Paper board, 20 min: the same cards printed with the source line on the face and
  teacher notes on a separate sheet (as L2.1 teacher prep, line 252, already does);
  a chart-paper mat with the two axes and the three eye labels; a "Can't tell"
  envelope; sticky notes for reasons, one per card. Groups of 3 to 4.
- Movement line, 3 to 5 min per axis: a tape line on the floor, one axis at a time.
  Students stand where their card belongs and say one reason. This is the workshop
  excerpt and the no-projector, no-internet route.
- More than one way in and out: cards read aloud by a partner or teacher, large-print
  set, placement by hand or by standing, reasons spoken, written, or pointed to on a
  printed chip sheet.
