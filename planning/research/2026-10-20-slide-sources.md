## S4 clouds

Heights are the WMO temperate-region levels: low is surface to 2 km, middle is 2 to 7 km, high is 5 to 13 km. Related claims are V1 and V2 in planning/VERIFY.md.

| Title | Publisher | URL | Supports |
|-------|-----------|-----|----------|
| International Cloud Atlas, "Levels" (Table 6) | World Meteorological Organization | https://cloudatlas.wmo.int/en/some-useful-concepts-levels.html | Height ranges for high, middle, and low clouds (temperate, polar, tropical). Altostratus often extends higher; nimbostratus usually reaches into other levels; cumulus and cumulonimbus have low bases but tops in the middle and high levels. |
| International Cloud Atlas, species Cumulus congestus | World Meteorological Organization | https://cloudatlas.wmo.int/en/species-cumulus-congestus-cu-con.html | Strongly sprouting cumulus of great vertical extent; cauliflower top; showers; often develops into cumulonimbus. |
| International Cloud Atlas, species lenticularis | World Meteorological Organization | https://cloudatlas.wmo.int/en/clouds-species-lenticularis.html | Lens or almond shape; most often orographic; applies mainly to cirrocumulus, altocumulus, and stratocumulus. |
| Ten Basic Clouds | NOAA National Weather Service JetStream | https://www.noaa.gov/jetstream/clouds/ten-basic-clouds | Appearance of each genus and the high, middle, and low groups; nimbostratus forms as altostratus thickens; altostratus virga; altocumulus corona; cirrostratus halo; cumulonimbus is the thunderstorm cloud reaching the high level; stratus can give drizzle; cumulus forms from daytime convection. | Cloud Types | UCAR Center for Science Education | https://scied.ucar.edu/learning-zone/clouds/cloud-types | Height groups in km and feet; vertical-growth clouds from the surface to 13 km (43,000 ft); lenticular as a cloud that forms over mountains.
