Part One · Carry Forward
Key Lesson

Weather is based on fundamental physics, here and throughout the universe. Pressure and water are always moving, never fixed or stuck at one height.

Key Takeaway

The same physics, gauges, and data points that predict tomorrow's weather also show patterns and change over many years. With enough data, we look ahead on momentum, not speculation.

  1. What did you learn more deeply about the weather?
  2. What changed about how you think about clouds, the atmosphere, or the water cycle?
  3. What is one question about the weather you want to find the answer to?

Part Two · The River as Teacher

The Salmon's Road Home

68 °F · 20 °C

Adult salmon begin to struggle in water warmer than about 68 °F. In the hot, low-snow summer of 2015, roughly a quarter million adult sockeye died in warm Columbia Basin water before they could spawn.

Part Two · Carry Forward
Key Lesson

Climate is social as much as it is scientific; society moves action much more than the science does. How we come to know things, and who we trust, shapes what we believe.

Key Takeaway

Narrative and emotion move us whether we notice it or not, so we learn to read both our sources and ourselves. Think about thinking.

  1. What did you learn about how we come to know things, and who we trust to share their knowledge with us?
  2. Was there an idea you held that changed or became more clear?
  3. What is one question about climate change that you want to follow up on?
Part Three
Adaptation,
Mitigation, Resilience

Used like one word. They are three different jobs, with different tools, often done by different people.

Part Three · Different Jobs, Different Tools

Three Jobs

Adaptation

Adjust to What Is Here and Coming

Change how we live, build, harvest, and plan so the impacts do less harm.

planninggroundworkpreparationdata
Mitigation

Reduce the Cause

Cut the gases warming the air and water, and keep carbon in forests, soils, and wetlands.

energyforestspolicyfunding
Resilience

Absorb, Recover, Carry Forward

The capacity of a people and a place to take a hit, come back, and keep their lifeways whole.

relationshipsknowledgereadinessemergency

We must adapt now if we are to be resilient. We must mitigate now if we are to limit the impacts on future generations.

Part Three · Carry Forward
Key Lesson

These three are not the same. They are different jobs with different tools: policy, groundwork, preparation, data, knowledge, funding, and sometimes emergency.

Key Takeaway

The impacts are already here, so we respond quickly and on purpose, without getting lost in fear, the future, and the black mirror of media.

  1. How do you now understand climate change adaptation, mitigation, and resilience?
  2. Did your sense of what needs to happen, or how fast, change today?
  3. What is one question about responding to climate impacts in your own community that you want to find out?
