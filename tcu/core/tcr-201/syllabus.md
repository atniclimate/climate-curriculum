## How to Use This Template

A syllabus and ten weekly lesson plans for a 201-level course on Tribal climate resilience as action, for any Tribal College or University (TCU) and any Northwest Indian College extended campus site, and usable at four-year institutions and as professional development for Tribal staff.

- **Four modules:** (1) Atmospheric Drivers of Land and Sea Impacts, Weeks 1 and 2; (2) Downscaling: From the Globe to the Reservation, Weeks 3 and 4; (3) Climate Action: Local Impacts, Readiness, and Resources, Weeks 5 to 7; (4) Planning and Collaboration, Weeks 8 to 10.
- **Shared core and local lessons:** the outcomes, signature assessments, rubrics, labs, the three jobs, and resilience as sovereignty are the same at every site. Each week names a local lesson socket where {{institution.name}} adds its own place-based lesson, taught by its own faculty and knowledge holders.
- **Two cases side by side:** the shared case is {{case.shared}}, carried from the source course so every site can compare notes; the local case is {{case.local}}.
- **Sequence:** TCR 201 follows Climate Change in Indian Country (CCIC 200), built on NESC 215 at Northwest Indian College, which ends with an adaptation plan outline. TCR 201 carries a plan into action: who decides, who pays, who else must be at the table, and what the work looks like on a Tuesday morning in a two-person department.
- **Format:** a 10-week quarter of 150-minute blocks, each splitting into two 75-minute sessions at the marked break, with a 15 to 16 week semester crosswalk.
- **Hand first, then tools:** every lab starts by hand from printed tables. Students rebuild NASA and NOAA visualizations from public data, first on paper, then in a spreadsheet. Tools support and never replace Tribal decision-making.
- **Data posture:** every data view is labeled. SNAPSHOT views come from the site's dated case pack; LIVE tools and sites (the Dynamic Drought Module, NOAA and NASA pages) are opened by the instructor or students outside class, never run in class; TEACHING MODEL views say so. A blank is unknown, never zero.
- **Worked example:** Module 2 reads this site's own projection card. Public data about a Nation's lands is not the Nation's voice (Week 4).
- **Markers:** `[PLACEHOLDER: ...]` marks what the host institution or instructor supplies; `[INSTRUCTOR SELECTS]` marks local readings; [LOCAL EXPERT: TCU faculty or knowledge holder] marks room for local teaching; [NEEDS TRIBAL CONFIRMATION: the Nation(s) whose homelands the campus is on] marks what only that Nation can supply or approve; [verify] marks details to confirm before teaching. Verification is part of the curriculum, not a gap in it.
- **Cultural content:** this course supplies no cultural teachings, stories, place names, or ceremonies. When a lesson calls for them, invite knowledge holders through the Nation's education or cultural resources department and follow its protocols.
- **No one speaks for a Nation:** no learner speaks or acts as a Nation, council, Elder, or knowledge holder. Learners work as analysts, planners, and staff; they may quote a Nation's own public words, short and cited.

## Course Description

Climate impacts are already here. This course is about what Tribal Nations do about them, and how. It starts in the atmosphere, because a Nation deciding what to protect needs to know what drives the change. Students watch NASA visualizations of carbon dioxide and methane, rebuild them from public data, and follow the heat to land and sea. Then they bring the signal to the ground through historic records, regional projections under two Shared Socioeconomic Pathway scenarios (SSP2-4.5 and SSP3-7.0) for {{region.name}}, with the Pacific Northwest as the shared case, and Tribal-scale projections, with a projection card any Nation can use.

The second half is action. Students assess heat, drought, fire, and the El Niño Southern Oscillation (ENSO), by hand from dated captures and with the ATNI Dynamic Drought Module where it covers their region; trace those hazards into infrastructure, emergency response, and a Nation's economy; and build a funding strategy by hand before checking it against a screening tool where one is available. They review a published Tribal plan, track policy, and read the land an action touches, with supporting tools where they cover the region. Finally, they practice coordination among jurisdictions and rights holders through the shared case, {{case.shared}} (from the withdrawn Resilient Columbia Basin Agreement to the Goldendale pumped storage case on sacred ground), and through the local case, {{case.local}}. Each student ends by presenting an Implementation Package to a review panel, or to a real council only by that council's own invitation.

Sovereignty is the organizing principle. Climate work is sovereignty work. No one can carry all of this work alone.

## Where This Course Sits in the Pathway

CCIC 200 is the primer: weather and climate, local impacts, ways of knowing, and sovereignty through InterTribal and international policy, in three rings (Ring 1: The Nation; Ring 2: InterTribal; Ring 3: International). TCR 201 is action: it puts the rings to work through policy watch, consultation, InterTribal resolutions, and coordination, with hand-first tool labs. Both sit in a pathway that runs from community and educator sessions (*Understanding a Climate of Change*), through Grades 6 to 12, to CCIC 200 and this course. All levels share one spine:

1. **Begin with experience, not abstraction:** a visualization, a data file, a plan, a docket, a flood.
2. **Plural ways of knowing:** Traditional Ecological Knowledge (TEK) and Indigenous Knowledges stand alongside Western science, on the Nation's terms.
3. **The Elder-and-child test:** plain enough to enter, deep enough to respect; the surest sign you understand something is being able to pass it on.
4. **Data literacy as sovereignty:** what data is useful, how it leads to action, and who gets to see it.
5. **From learning to leading:** CCIC 200 ends with a plan outline; TCR 201 ends with a decision brief in front of a review panel.
6. **Relationships matter:** it is going to take all of us.

## Learning Outcomes

By the end of this course, students will be able to:

1. **Explain** the sources and measurement of carbon dioxide and methane, and rebuild at least one NASA or NOAA visualization from public data.
2. **Interpret** how added heat reaches land and sea (anomalies and baselines, land versus ocean, sea ice, sea level) and connect it to the waters, coasts, lands, and First Foods of their region, with the Pacific Northwest as the shared case.
3. **Explain** downscaling, bias correction, ensembles, and Shared Socioeconomic Pathways (SSPs); read projections under SSP2-4.5 and SSP3-7.0; and explain why even downscaled data differ for Tribes.
4. **Communicate** one climate signal from global to local in plain language, every figure sourced and dated, the scenario named, and uncertainty stated.
5. **Assess** a Nation's observed and projected heat, drought, fire, and ENSO impacts and its infrastructure, emergency, and economic exposure, and distinguish adaptation, mitigation, and resilience.
6. **Construct** a verified funding strategy (sources, eligibility, match, indirect costs, capacity, risks, braiding) that translates between "climate" and "natural hazard" language without losing the Nation's purpose.
7. **Analyze** a published Tribal plan, the policy that governs an action, and the land it touches, by hand first and then with tools.
8. **Map** the jurisdictions, rights holders, and partners an action touches, and analyze consultation, InterTribal resolutions, and negotiation grounded in reserved rights, the trust responsibility, and self-determination.
9. **Apply** the CARE Principles (Collective benefit, Authority to control, Responsibility, and Ethics), with OCAP (Ownership, Control, Access, and Possession) studied as a First Nations framework, and the Tiered Sovereignty Data Framework (TSDF) to decide what data can be shared, with whom, and how.
10. **Present** an Implementation Package with a one-page decision brief to a council-style review panel in five minutes, designed around turnover, grant cliffs, emergencies, and burnout.

## Instructional Approach

- **Rebuild before you trust:** watch a visualization, then rebuild it from the numbers. If you can rebuild the picture, you can explain it to an Elder and a child and spot when someone else's picture is wrong.
- **Compared to what:** every number carries its baseline, date, and source; every projection its scenario and period.
- **Hand first, then tools:** tools change, get defunded, or go offline, so the method must transfer without them. These tools support; they never replace Tribal decision-making.
- **Verify every term; cut tedium that doesn't matter:** programs, rules, courts, and data feeds changed repeatedly in 2025 and 2026. Verify from primary sources and record the date. Put effort into work that protects the Nation (accurate reporting, consultation records, data governance).
- **Three eyes:** each week names its scale: Frog's Eye (up close, at the water), Bird's Eye (the watershed, region, plans, and partners), Moon's Eye (the whole planet).
- **Reflection without ratings:** three open questions each week, read for content and completion, not scored, and one "say it plain" line.
- **Every session moves through four parts** (NESC 215): Presence, Application, Inquiry, and Reflection.

## Signature Assessments and Rubrics

### Signature Assessment 1: Climate Signal Brief (Modules 1 and 2)

**What it is:** a two-page brief tracing one climate signal (for example days at or above a heat threshold, first snow, frozen precipitation, lake or sea ice, or sea level for a coastal Nation) from the globe to the ground for one Nation or case. It must pass the Elder-and-child test.

**Required elements:**

- **The signal:** why it matters, using the Nation's priorities only as publicly described or as a knowledge holder has approved.
- **Global:** one NASA global visualization or dataset, with its date.
- **Regional:** one downscaled projection for your region (the Pacific Northwest in the shared case), with scenario, period, and baseline.
- **Tribal scale:** one Tribal-scale projection (your projection card) and what its boundary leaves out.
- **Local observation:** one observed record (weather station, stream gauge, snow telemetry, the Community Collaborative Rain, Hail and Snow Network (CoCoRaHS), gridMET, or Parameter-elevation Regressions on Independent Slopes Model (PRISM) data, where each covers your region) or a community observation shared with permission and credited as the person chooses.
- **Uncertainty:** what is well known, what differs across models and scenarios, and what the data cannot say.
- **Figures:** at least one student-made lab figure; every figure sourced, dated, and labeled SNAPSHOT or TEACHING MODEL.

| Criterion | Exemplary (4) | Proficient (3) | Developing (2) | Emerging (1) |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Signal chain | Global, regional, Tribal, and local pieces form one story | Connected with a gap | Four separate facts | A scale missing |
| Accuracy and sourcing | Every figure accurate, sourced, dated; student figure honest and labeled | Minor gaps | Several unsourced or undated | Errors or invented figures |
| Scenario, baseline, uncertainty | Named every time; uncertainty stated plainly | Named most of the time | Inconsistent | Projection presented as prediction |
| Elder-and-child readability | Plain, warm, every term clear | One or two unexplained terms | Technical in places | Jargon-heavy |
| Sovereignty and permission | Permission for community knowledge; boundary limits named | Mostly careful | Speaks for the Nation in places | Knowledge used without permission, or boundaries presented as jurisdiction |

### Signature Assessment 2: Local Impacts and Readiness Profile (Module 3, Weeks 5 and 6)

**What it is:** a three to four page profile setting what is observed now beside what is projected, then asking whether infrastructure, emergency systems, and the economy are ready.

**Required elements:**

- **Evidence view:** a Dynamic Drought Module view, opened outside class where the tool covers your region and saved as a shareable URL, with the hazard cluster and horizon chosen and why, a dated screenshot, what the impact briefing says, and a cross-check with one other source (the U.S. Drought Monitor, CoCoRaHS, or a National Weather Service (NWS) product). Where the tool does not cover your Nation or case, a dated capture of another public drought or hazard product, with the reason.
- **Projection:** the relevant Module 2 projection with scenario and period, and one paragraph on what it could mean if this season's conditions became common.
- **Exposure table (Template H):** infrastructure, emergency, and economic exposure for the top hazards, with sources, unknowns, the responsible department, and current readiness.
- **Gaps and a candidate action:** the three most important gaps, stated respectfully, and one candidate action labeled by which job it does (adaptation, mitigation, or resilience).

| Criterion | Exemplary (4) | Proficient (3) | Developing (2) | Emerging (1) |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Evidence view | Purposeful view; briefing read accurately; cross-checked | Cross-check thin | Screenshot without interpretation | Missing |
| Observed versus projected | Distinct and clearly connected | Mostly distinct | Blurred | Confused |
| Exposure table | Complete, sourced, unknowns named, responsibility assigned; public information only | Mostly complete | Thin or unsourced | Missing |
| Economic realism | Direct costs, lost revenue, and shifted costs identified | Some identified | Mentioned only | Ignored |
| Gaps and action | Three reasoned gaps; action tied to evidence | Present | Loosely linked | Missing |

### Signature Assessment 3: Funding Strategy Matrix (Week 7)

**What it is:** tables and a two-page narrative showing how to pay for one action over its full life, not just its first year.

**Required elements:**

- **Hazard-to-program links:** a hand-built table linking the Nation's top three hazards (from the Readiness Profile) to programs. Where a screening tool such as the TCR Policy Scanner is available, compare its output: where it agrees, where it differs, and which you trust for what. Without a tool, the hand-built table stands alone.
- **Sources:** at least five across at least three categories (federal, state, philanthropic, Tribal own-source, partner or in-kind), each with eligibility, status verified this term (date and URL), award range, match, indirect cost treatment, window, reporting burden, and fit with the Nation's priorities.
- **Braiding or stacking plan:** how sources combine without double-counting, and without federal funds matching other federal funds unless a statute allows it.
- **Capacity line:** who writes, manages, and reports. If the answer is "one person who already has three jobs," say so and address it.
- **Risks and timeline:** a fallback if the lead source is cut, paused, or reframed, and a 12 to 36 month calendar from application to closeout.

| Criterion | Exemplary (4) | Proficient (3) | Developing (2) | Emerging (1) |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Hazard-to-program links | Sound links; tool comparison weighs currency and fit | Comparison thin | Links assumed | Missing |
| Verified landscape and eligibility | Every source verified with date and link; eligibility checked line by line | Most verified | Some outdated or assumed | Expired or ineligible programs |
| Match, indirect, and compliance | Allowable match; indirect handled; 2 CFR 200 basics applied | Addressed | Mentioned only | Ignored |
| Braiding and sequencing | Allowable braid or stack, no double-counting | Reasonable | Listed, not combined | Single source |
| Capacity and risk | Capacity gap addressed; fallback for each major risk | Both addressed | One addressed | Neither |

### Signature Assessment 4: Plan, Policy, and Land Review (assigned Week 8; due end of Week 9)

**What it is:** three linked parts answering what every funder and council asks before an action moves: does it fit the Nation's plans, what policy governs it, and whose land does it touch?

- **Part A: Plan review.** Review one published Tribal climate adaptation plan (CAP) or hazard mitigation plan with the course rubric (Template C): an assessment, not a grade, centered on the Nation's stated goals. Include a short summary, the rubric with page citations, three gaps stated respectfully, three actions that could start within 12 months with a likely lead, and an alignment note against one federal program (for example, Federal Emergency Management Agency (FEMA) Tribal mitigation plan requirements), noting that a departure may be the Nation's choice.
- **Part B: Policy watch list.** At least eight items (Template D) across federal rules, funding notices, state bills, consultation invitations, InterTribal resolutions, litigation, and regional agreements, each with a recommended response for the Nation's staff to consider (monitor, brief council, comment, request consultation, coordinate with {{region.intertribal}} or a national InterTribal organization such as the National Congress of American Indians (NCAI), or no action). In the shared case, the source course also named ATNI and the Columbia River Inter-Tribal Fish Commission (CRITFC).
- **Part C: Land-use and jurisdiction note (one to two pages)** for one action: who owns and governs the land (trust, fee, federal, state, private); which land use rules apply; what a county assessor record can and cannot tell you; likely permits `[verify with the Nation's staff or counsel]`; and who must be consulted. Describe ceded lands and usual and accustomed areas only as the Nation does.
- **Tool comparison (where available and where it covers your region):** one paragraph comparing hand work with a plan, policy, or land tool.

| Criterion | Exemplary (4) | Proficient (3) | Developing (2) | Emerging (1) |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Plan accuracy and stance | Every finding cited; read on the Nation's terms | Most cited; mostly respectful | Few citations; grades against outside norms | Unsupported or dismissive |
| Gaps and actions | Three significant gaps; three concrete actions with leads | Some thin | Vague | Missing |
| Watch list | Eight or more real items; accurate dates; clear triage | Minor gaps | Fewer items or inaccurate | Invented items |
| Land and jurisdiction | Ownership, governance, and record limits accurate; boundaries handled with care | Mostly accurate | General | Missing, or boundaries presented as jurisdiction |
| Legal and relational grounding | Treaty and other reserved rights, trust responsibility, and consultation cited accurately | Mostly accurate | General references | Missing or wrong |

### Signature Assessment 5: Capstone Implementation Package (Weeks 9 and 10)

**What it is:** a complete package for one action from the student's own Nation's plan (with permission, and only public information unless the Nation has approved otherwise), from the local case ({{case.local}}), from the shared case ({{case.shared}}), or from another public Tribal plan. The package is the student's own analysis; it is never presented as any Nation's position.

**Required sections (10 to 14 pages plus appendices):**

1. **Action and evidence:** what, where, why, what it protects, and the evidence from the revised Signal Brief and Readiness Profile.
2. **Lead department and partners:** who would own it inside the Nation and why, as public sources describe the Nation's structure; each partner's role.
3. **Coordination map (Template G):** every jurisdiction, rights holder, and partner the action touches; who decides what; where consultation or negotiation is needed; what is not on the table.
4. **Decision pathway and one-page decision brief (Template A):** who decides, and when (council calendar, budget cycle, grant window, seasonal round).
5. **Funding, policy, and land:** the revised Matrix and Review, including consultation needed.
6. **Data and sovereignty plan:** data used and produced, TSDF tier (decided by the Nation), owner, access, and agreements.
7. **Monitoring and evaluation:** indicators, including a place for one of the Nation's own measures of health, as the Nation names it, and how results return to council and community.
8. **Real-world risks and continuity:** turnover, grant cliffs, emergencies, and burnout, with mitigations, plus the Week 6 "if I leave tomorrow" page.
9. **Presentation:** five minutes, council-style, with up to five minutes of questions, to a review panel (instructor, invited guests who speak as themselves, classmates as reviewers), or to a real council only by its own invitation. Never a mock council played by learners.

|---|---|---|---|---|
| Action and evidence | Tied to the plan, the Nation's priorities, and sourced evidence | Minor gaps | Loosely tied | Not tied |
| Coordination map | Every party mapped; authority and negotiation points accurate | Mostly complete | Generic | Missing |
| Decision pathway and brief | Accurate pathway; brief council-ready and Elder-and-child readable | Mostly accurate | Generic | Missing |
| Funding strategy | Verified, braided, realistic, capacity-aware | Minor gaps | Thin or unverified | Missing |
| Data, monitoring, and evaluation | Tiers, ownership, and agreements clear; the Nation's own measures included | Mostly clear | General | Absent or unsafe |
| Real-world realism | Designs around turnover, cliffs, emergencies, and burnout | Names most | Names one | Ignores |
| Presentation | Clear in five minutes; handles questions with respect | Minor overrun | Unfocused | Unprepared |

## Tools: What Each One Is, and Where It Covers

Every tool lab starts by hand; without a tool, that station runs fully by hand. Tool facts below come from each tool's own README (read 10/15/2026); check again before teaching.

- **ATNI Dynamic Drought Module** (LIVE; opened outside class): an embeddable web map of drought, wildfire, and extreme heat across North America, wherever the issuing agencies publish data. Its README states coverage is rich for the United States and limited for Canada and Mexico. Some station networks it reads are regional. Use it where it covers your region.
- **TCR Policy Scanner:** a public-data climate physical-risk screening tool that builds per-Nation screening profiles from public federal data. Its README describes it as development grade, "not validated science and not a public release," and states it makes no policy, funding, risk, financial, prioritization, or eligibility decision; those belong to each Nation. Where it is available, students compare it against their hand-built table. No per-Nation output, rank, or packet is copied into coursework from this template. `[PLACEHOLDER: current access contact for ATNI Climate tools]`
- **Policy Sentinel:** a policy monitoring and source-reference engine. Its README says its source catalog describes Washington, Oregon, Idaho, Alaska, California, Montana, and Nevada services and some InterTribal services, and that its ordinary build is synthetic; it is in development. Use it where it covers your region; elsewhere the policy watch runs by hand.
- **Plan Assessor and the Land Use Analyzer:** described in the source course (Week 8); check their current access and coverage before teaching [verify].

## Course Policies

- **Family, ceremony, and seasonal responsibilities:** absences for ceremony, funerals, family needs, and fishing, hunting, gathering, and harvest seasons are respected, with no documentation of ceremony required. Missed work is made up by agreement.
- **Presence:** here in mind as well as body. `[PLACEHOLDER: institutional attendance policy]`
- **Climate maturity:** this course deals with loss, disasters, and broken commitments. Grief, anger, and fatigue are normal; you may step out of any discussion. `[PLACEHOLDER: campus counseling and community wellbeing resources]`
- **Knowledge shared in the room:** what guests and classmates share about their families, communities, and Nations stays in the room unless they give permission. Do not record guests without permission.
- **Late work:** communicate early. The capstone date is fixed because guests plan around it.
- **Claims and sources:** every claim needs a source; program, rule, court, and data status need the date checked.
- **Artificial intelligence (AI) tools:** allowed for drafting or checking if you verify every fact and disclose use. Never put Tribal internal documents, culturally sensitive information, or data above T0 into a public AI tool. `[PLACEHOLDER: institutional AI policy]`
- **Speaking for Nations:** never present your work as the position of a Nation, InterTribal organization, or agency. No student speaks or acts as a Nation, council, Elder, or knowledge holder. Practice letters, briefs, and resolution language are labeled as classroom exercises written by students as analysts or staff.
- **Student data:** the course collects no student data beyond what the institution requires; any tool students use in a browser keeps their work on their own device.

## Working With Tribes Respectfully

- **Ask the local Nation first.** Before inviting a guest, using a Nation's plan or data in depth, or visiting a site, contact the Nation's education, cultural resources, or natural resources department and follow its protocols. No response is not permission.
- **Permission from some is not permission from all.** Approval from one leader, department, or family is not approval from the Nation, and approval from one Nation is not approval from another.
- **Compensate knowledge holders.** Elders, knowledge holders, and Tribal staff are paid for their time and credited as they choose. `[PLACEHOLDER: institutional honoraria process]`
- **Do not invent or borrow cultural content.** Teachings, stories, place names, and ceremonies come from the Nation, through its people, on its terms.
- **Respect Local Contexts labels, CARE, and OCAP.** Traditional Knowledge (TK) and Biocultural (BC) Labels (https://localcontexts.org/) are created and applied by Indigenous communities; respect any label a community has applied, and never apply one yourself. The CARE Principles (Global Indigenous Data Alliance) guide how Indigenous data is gathered, held, and used. OCAP is a First Nations framework (First Nations Information Governance Centre), studied here for what it teaches, not as a rule for every U.S. Tribe.
- **Boundaries are not jurisdiction maps.** Do not present Treaty, ceded, usual and accustomed, Census, or data-product boundaries as definitive jurisdiction without the Nation.
- **Language:** Tribal citizens are rights holders, relatives, community members, and partners, never "stakeholders."
- **Do your homework before you ask.** Tribal staff are busy.

## Data Sovereignty Note

- **Public data first.** Coursework uses public (T0) data unless a Nation has given written permission. Public projections about a Nation's lands are T0; what a Nation adds is tiered by the Nation.
- **Tier every dataset.** Every capstone assigns each dataset a TSDF tier (T0 Rain, T1 Lake, T2 River, T3 Canoe). The Nation decides.
- **No culturally sensitive data in coursework.** T3 information stays out of papers, slides, and shared drives; describe a dependency on it without including it.
- **Data returns to the Nation.** Anything produced with a Nation's permission is offered back to it.
- **Labels on every view.** LIVE, SNAPSHOT, or TEACHING MODEL, with source and date. A blank is unknown, never zero.

## Glossary

- **Adaptation:** reducing harm from climate change already underway or expected.
- **Anomaly:** the difference between a value and the average over a baseline period.
- **Baseline:** the reference period for an anomaly or change (1850 to 1900, 1951 to 1980, 1950 to 1999). Always name it.
- **Bias correction:** adjusting model output so its historic period matches observations.
- **Braiding and stacking:** combining sources on one project while tracking each separately; layering sources over phases.
- **Climate adaptation plan (CAP):** a Nation's plan of values, vulnerabilities, priorities, actions, and monitoring for climate impacts.
- **Co-benefit and maladaptation:** a second good from one action; an action that increases vulnerability elsewhere, later, or for someone else.
- **Co-management:** government-to-government management of shared relatives and places, as under U.S. v. Oregon in the shared case.
- **Consultation, collaboration, consent:** an agency seeking and considering a Nation's views before acting, without requiring agreement; shared work toward shared goals; the Nation can say no and the answer holds.
- **Downscaling:** translating global model output to finer scales, **statistically** (from observed relationships) or **dynamically** (a regional model inside a global one).
- **El Niño Southern Oscillation (ENSO):** a recurring tropical Pacific pattern with warm El Niño and cool La Niña phases that shifts weather far away, including winters in many regions (in the shared case, the Pacific Northwest).
- **Ensemble:** a set of model runs that shows the range of possible outcomes.
- **Free, prior, and informed consent (FPIC):** the UNDRIP standard (Articles 19 and 32) that Indigenous Peoples' consent be sought freely, before a decision, with full information.
- **Global mean sea level (GMSL):** the worldwide average height of the ocean surface, measured by satellite since 1993.
- **Grant cliff:** when a grant ends and what it funded ends with it.
- **Hazard mitigation plan:** a plan to reduce hazard risk; FEMA approval (44 CFR 201.7) opens many mitigation funds and requires updates every five years.
- **HeatRisk:** an NWS rating of heat-related health risk for the next seven days.
- **Indirect cost rate and match:** a project's share of the Nation's administrative costs (2 CFR 200.414); the recipient's share of project costs (2 CFR 200.306).
- **Mitigation:** reducing emissions or storing carbon. "Hazard mitigation" in emergency management means reducing hazard risk. Know which a program means.
- **North American Drought Monitor:** a tri-national consensus drought map for Canada, the United States, and Mexico, via NOAA NCEI.
- **Projection card:** a one-page record (Template I) of a Tribal-scale projection: the unit and its Census name, scenarios and periods, what the boundary leaves out, and who should see it first.
- **Reserved rights:** rights Tribes kept in treaties, not rights granted to them (U.S. v. Winans, 1905).
- **Resilience:** a Nation's capacity to absorb, recover from, and adapt to change while keeping its relationships, responsibilities, and self-determination.
- **Sea surface height anomaly (SSHA):** how far the ocean surface stands above or below its long-term average at a place and time.
- **Shared Socioeconomic Pathway (SSP):** a scenario pairing a story of world development with a forcing level by 2100: SSP2-4.5 (middle of the road), SSP3-7.0 (high emissions, fragmented world). An "if," not a forecast.
- **SNAPSHOT, LIVE, TEACHING MODEL:** a dated capture in the site's case pack; a live public site, opened outside class; invented or simplified numbers that show a pattern and say so.
- **Traditional Ecological Knowledge (TEK):** knowledge of relationships among living beings and their environment, carried across generations by Indigenous Peoples; part of Indigenous Knowledges.
- **Trust responsibility:** federal legal and moral obligations to Tribal Nations arising from treaties, statutes, and the government-to-government relationship.
- **Usual and accustomed places:** places where Treaty Tribes reserved the right to fish, including off-reservation.
