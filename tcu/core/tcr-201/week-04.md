## Key lesson

Even good downscaled data differ for Tribes, because the boundaries, grid cells, elevations, and variables that data products use were not chosen by Tribes. A Tribal-scale projection is useful when the Nation knows what it leaves out.

## Key takeaway

Read the boundary before you read the numbers. The data are a starting point; the Nation decides which questions they answer.

## Learning objectives

Students will be able to:

- Explain four reasons downscaled projections differ for Tribes.
- Describe the methods behind the Native Climate projections.
- Read a projection card (Template I) and fill a table for SSP2-4.5 and SSP3-7.0 at mid-century and end of century, naming page, chart, and how sure each read is.
- Build a projection card for the Nation or case they follow, including the Tiered Sovereignty Data Framework (TSDF) tier of any local data added, with the tier decided by the Nation.

## Check for Understanding

1. Name two of the four reasons downscaled projections differ for Tribes. Say them, or point to them on a printed map with a grid drawn over a boundary.
   Listen for: elevation gets averaged away; grid cells are coarse; boundaries are someone else's (Census or BIA lines are not ceded lands or usual and accustomed areas); variables are someone else's.
2. Your card's report uses a Census name. Why does that matter?
   Listen for: the data product named the unit, not the Nation; a different name or a missing Nation is a data sovereignty finding.
3. Who decides the TSDF tier of a local weather station record the Nation adds to the card?
   Listen for: the Nation, always; public projections about its lands are T0, but what the Nation adds is tiered by the Nation.

## Instructor notes

- **About the source course.** The source course (curriculum/TCR-201) used one Columbia River Basin Nation's public reservation projections as its worked example, because the course is anchored in the Columbia River Basin and a public report exists. Whether that Nation was informed of, or consulted on, that use is an open item in the source. This template does not carry that example. Any Nation-specific worked example requires that Nation's confirmation: [NEEDS TRIBAL CONFIRMATION: <Nation>].
- Using public data about a Nation's lands is not working with the Nation: do not present student tables as any Nation's assessment, and do not speculate about what a Nation should do.
- **Mini-lecture: why even downscaled data differ for Tribes.** (1) Elevation gets averaged away: a reservation that runs from a valley floor to mountain slopes hides both the valley's heat and the mountain's snow in one average. (2) Grid cells are coarse: at 0.25 degree (roughly 25 kilometers), a small reservation or scattered trust parcels may fall in one or two cells. (3) Boundaries are someone else's: Census or Bureau of Indian Affairs (BIA) polygons are not ceded lands or usual and accustomed areas; a Nation's rights and relatives reach past the reservation line. (4) Variables are someone else's: a Nation's observations and priorities (First Foods, seasons) may call for different variables and thresholds than agricultural metrics.
- **Mini-lecture: Native Climate.** Native Climate is led by the Desert Research Institute with U.S. Department of Agriculture (USDA) National Institute of Food and Agriculture funding, working with Tribal Extension at the University of Nevada, Reno and the University of Arizona, the Montana Climate Office, and the USDA Climate Hubs. Projections use eight CMIP6 models downscaled with NEX-GDDP-CMIP6, Census TIGER/Line boundaries, a historical period of 1950 to 2014, projections from 2015 to 2100, and four SSPs, across about 22 agricultural metrics. They cover 633 units nationally (reservations, Tribal and Alaska Native statistical areas, and Hawaiian climate divisions), named by Census name. Report pattern: `https://data.climate.umt.edu/native-climate/projections/pdf/[Census name]_CMIP6-climate-projections.pdf` (spaces appear as `%20`); data archive: `[Census name].zip` in the projections directory. Tribal College archives: https://data.climate.umt.edu/native-climate/tcu/ holds 35 Tribal College data archives with undocumented contents. `[PLACEHOLDER: ATNI Climate to explore and document the TCU archives before classroom use]`

## Sovereignty thread

Reading the boundary first is data sovereignty: the Nation, not the data product, decides which questions its numbers answer and who sees what it adds (Ring 1: The Nation). The CARE Principles name that authority to control in the international layer (Ring 3: International).
