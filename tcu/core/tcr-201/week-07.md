## Key lesson

Money is not the only bottleneck. Capacity is. A grant a Nation cannot staff, match, manage, or report on can cost more than it brings. Start with the Nation's hazards, priorities, and capacity, not whatever funding notice is open.

## Key takeaway

Verify everything, every term. Programs are cut, paused, renamed, restarted by courts, and reframed. The Nation's purpose stays the same.

## Learning objectives

Students will be able to:

- Link hazards to program eligibility by hand, then compare with a tool where one is available, verifying status from primary sources.
- Explain match, indirect cost rates, braiding and stacking, and the basics of 2 CFR Part 200 [verify].
- Reframe an action for a program's language without changing what it does.

## Hands-on activity

**Mini-lecture notes: the landscape and its volatility.** Categories: federal (for example BIA, FEMA, Environmental Protection Agency (EPA) programs including the Indian Environmental General Assistance Program, the National Oceanic and Atmospheric Administration (NOAA), the Department of Energy (DOE) Office of Indian Energy, the U.S. Department of Agriculture (USDA), the Army Corps of Engineers, and the Bureau of Reclamation); state; philanthropic; partner and in-kind; and Tribal own-source. `[INSTRUCTOR SELECTS]` current programs, verified at the time of teaching. Then the 2025 to 2026 volatility the source course documents, which students check for changes this term:

- **FEMA Building Resilient Infrastructure and Communities (BRIC):** FEMA announced ending BRIC in April 2025; a federal court ordered it restarted; in 2026 FEMA described "reconstituting" BRIC with a new funding opportunity (advisory, 03/18/2026) [verify].
- **BIA TCR Annual Awards (formerly Tribal Climate Resilience):** the source course reports that the BIA page listed FY24 as the most recent cycle [verify].
- **EPA Environmental and Climate Justice Block Grants:** the source course reports these Inflation Reduction Act grants were terminated in 2025, that Tribes are among the plaintiffs in *Appalachian Voices et al. v. EPA*, and that the case was dismissed in 08/2025 and argued at the D.C. Circuit on 03/16/2026 [verify].
- **Language:** Many federal materials were reframed from "climate" to "natural hazard and disaster preparedness" under 2025 to 2026 directives [verify].

Never tell students a program is open unless you verified it that week.

**Mechanics (verify this term):** an active System for Award Management (SAM.gov) registration; match under 2 CFR 200.306 (federal funds generally cannot match another federal award unless a statute allows it; ask the grants office or counsel); indirect costs under 2 CFR 200.414 (a negotiated rate or the de minimis rate the source course reports as 15 percent after the 2024 revision [verify]); braiding, blending, and stacking (never charge one cost twice); reporting, closeout, and the single audit threshold the source course reports as $1,000,000 after the 2024 revision [verify]. Plan the grant cliff on day one.

**Lab: Hazards to eligibility.**

1. **By hand first.** For the top three hazards in your Readiness Profile, list programs that could fund action. Groups split the programs and verify each from the issuer's own page: status, date and address checked, the words the agency uses, Tribal eligibility, match, and a public contact. The class builds one shared, dated landscape table (Template B, first table). Shared case first ({{case.shared}}), then this place (Local lesson).
2. **Then the tool, where available.** The TCR Policy Scanner (ATNI Climate) is a public-data climate physical-risk screening tool. By its own README (read 10/15/2026), it builds screening profiles for federally recognized Tribal Nations from public federal data only, is development grade, is "not validated science and not a public release," and "makes no policy, funding, risk, financial, prioritization, or eligibility decision"; those judgments belong to each Tribal Nation. Where ATNI Climate makes it available to the class, compare its view with your hand-built table. A screening profile about a Nation's lands is public data about that Nation, not the Nation's voice; it stays in class and is not posted or shared.
3. **Compare.** Where do your links and the tool agree or differ, and what did each miss? A tool is only as current as its last update; verify before submitting anything. Without the tool, the hand-built table stands alone and the lab is complete.

**Activity: Reframing without losing the purpose.** Rewrite your action for a program that uses natural hazard language. Describing the same real hazard in the program's words is translation; changing what the project does, or hiding the Nation's purpose, is not. A heat and smoke shelter is hazard mitigation and climate adaptation at once. Say what is true.

**Hand twin and other ways in:** The whole lab runs on paper from printed SNAPSHOTs of program pages. Students may sort program cards into hazard columns on a table before writing, and may present the reframing aloud as two sentences side by side.

## Check for Understanding

1. Why can a grant cost a Nation more than it brings?
   Listen for: match, indirect costs that land on the general fund, staff time, reporting, and the cliff when the grant ends.
2. Your hand table and a tool disagree about one program. What do you do before you write it in your Matrix?
   Listen for: check the issuer's own page this term, record the date and address, and say which source was more current.
3. A program uses "natural hazard" words. Show one honest way to describe your action in those words.
   Listen for: the same real hazard and the same project in the program's words, with the Nation's purpose still visible; not a changed project.

## Sovereignty thread

Ring 1: The Nation. Funding follows the Nation's own hazards, priorities, and capacity; a Nation may decline any grant that would bend its purpose. Ring 2: InterTribal. Nations share what they learn about programs and press for fair terms together through {{region.intertribal}} and national bodies, each speaking for itself.
