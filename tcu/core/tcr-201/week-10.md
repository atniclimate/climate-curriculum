## Key lesson

Relationships matter. It's going to take all of us. We can do more together than any of us can alone, and that includes our relationship with our non-human relatives.

## Key takeaway

We are not apart from nature; we are a part of nature. Be a good relative, be a good ancestor. Our world is changing rapidly, but we have all that we need, and we are sovereign, and we decide what matters.

## Learning objectives

Students will be able to:

- Present an Implementation Package in five minutes, leading with the one-page decision brief.
- Answer reviewers with clarity, honesty, and respect, including saying "that is the Nation's decision" or "I don't know yet" when that is true.
- Reflect on what they will carry forward, and to whom.

## Hands-on activity

**Capstone presentations (up to 12 presenters).** Presenters hand over the one-page decision brief (Template A) first, then present the Implementation Package (Signature Assessment 5) in five minutes. Every number on a slide carries its source and date; every data view carries its label (LIVE, SNAPSHOT, or TEACHING MODEL); a projection says "projection, not a prediction."

**Review panel guidance.** No one plays a council, a Nation, or an Elder. Guests ask as themselves. Classmates review with the capstone rubric. The panel asks the questions a decision-maker would need answered, from a printed question card:

- What does it cost? Who does the work?
- What happens when the grant ends?
- Who else must agree?
- Have the Elders been asked, and through what protocol? (A presenter does not answer for Elders; the right answer says whether and how the Nation's own process was followed.)
- Does this protect the Nation's rights, or trade them?
- What decision is needed, from whom, and when?

Each presenter gets one written strength and one question from each reviewer.

**Closing circle.** Each person answers in a sentence or two: What will you carry forward, and to whom will you pass it? `[PERSONAL VOICE: Patrick writes this]`

**Hand twin and other ways in:** A presentation can run from the printed brief alone, with no projector. A student may present with a partner reading, with a poster or a hand-drawn map, or in another accessible form agreed with the instructor; reviewers may give feedback aloud or in writing.

## Check for Understanding

1. In one breath, what decision does your brief ask for, and from whom?
   Listen for: one specific decision, one named decision-maker, and a time.
2. A reviewer asks a question only the Nation can answer. What do you say?
   Listen for: "that is the Nation's decision," and who in the Nation would be asked and how; no guessing on the Nation's behalf.
3. Name one way your action is built to survive turnover or a grant cliff.
   Listen for: a continuity page, shared files, a second person trained, a fallback funding source, or a phased plan.
