## Key lesson

It is going to take all of us. We can do more together than any of us can alone, and that includes our relationships with our non-human relatives.

## Key takeaway

We are not apart from nature, we are a part of nature. Go outside, pay attention, be a good relative, be a good ancestor. Our world is changing rapidly, but we have all that we need, and we are sovereign, and we decide what matters.

## Learning objectives

Students will be able to:

- Present a final product that connects climate science, ways of knowing, data, and community needs.
- Give and receive constructive peer feedback.
- Explain their work so that both an Elder and a child can understand it.
- Name their next steps in learning, community, and career.

## Hands-on activity

**Completing the Circle.** Students return to their Week 1 half-circle and draw the second half: what they hope and plan for the rest of their lives, particularly as it relates to climate, community, and career. Plans will change; that is expected. Drawing, words, or both are welcome. Optional: pair and share one piece.

**Presentations.** Each student presents their own final product as themselves (see the syllabus's final product options and rubric), including the one-minute explanation that both an Elder and a child could understand. Peer feedback names one strength and one question.

## Check for Understanding

1. In one minute, explain your final product so that both an Elder and a child could follow it. Say it, show a drawing, or read a short card.
   Listen for: one concern, one change in climate, one thing that could be done, in plain words with no unexplained jargon.
2. What is one thing your project is built on from science, and one thing from another way of knowing, and how did you get permission for the second?
   Listen for: a named data source; a way of knowing used only with permission and attributed as agreed, or a plain "I chose not to include any."
3. What is your next step, and who will you take it with?
   Listen for: a concrete step (TCR 201, a community group, an internship, keeping the weather journal or joining CoCoRaHS) and a person or group, not a solo plan.

- Guests in the audience hear the work as themselves. No student presents as, or speaks for, a Nation, a council, an Elder, or a knowledge holder; a student may quote a Nation's own public words, short and cited.
- Do not post or publish products that carry knowledge from Listen and Learn. Keep the course evaluation answers without names unless a student asks otherwise.

## Sovereignty thread

"We are sovereign, and we decide what matters." This week closes CCIC 200's primer and opens TCR 201.

- **Ring 1, the Nation:** every final product names who decides. A student's plan outline is a draft offered to a community, not a decision made for it.
- **Ring 2, InterTribal:** InterTribal organizations such as {{region.intertribal}} are where Nations of a region carry shared climate positions [verify]. TCR 201 teaches students to read and track those positions.
- **Ring 3, International:** the international layer (the UN Declaration on the Rights of Indigenous Peoples, the climate convention and its Local Communities and Indigenous Peoples Platform, the CARE Principles) gives words a Nation may choose to use; it never replaces the Nation's own voice.

The bridge: TCR 201 puts the rings to work through policy watch, consultation, resolutions, and coordination. See the primer, "Putting the rings to work" and "Questions to carry."
