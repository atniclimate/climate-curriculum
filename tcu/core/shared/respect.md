# Respect and Data Care

Module author: Patrick A. Freeland (module author), with ATNI Climate.

This page travels with every course page on this site. It says how the courses work with Tribal Nations, knowledge holders, and their information, in plain words. It is adapted from the respect and data care sections of the Climate Change in Indian Country (CCIC 200) and Tribal Climate Resilience (TCR 201) syllabi.

## Working with Tribes and knowledge holders respectfully

- **Ask the Nation first.** Before inviting a guest, using a Nation's plan or data in depth, or visiting a site, contact the Nation's education, cultural resources, or natural resources department and follow its protocols for guests, recording, and sharing.
- **Permission from some is not permission from all.** Approval from one leader, department, or family is not approval from the Nation, and approval from one Nation is not approval from another. When in doubt, ask again, and ask the right people.
- **No answer is not a yes.** Silence is not consent.
- **Pay and credit knowledge holders.** Elders, knowledge holders, and Tribal staff are paid for their time and credited as they choose. Budget for this before the term starts. `[PLACEHOLDER: institutional honoraria process]`
- **Do not invent or borrow cultural content.** These courses supply no teachings, stories, place names in a language, ceremonies, or cultural relationships to plants, animals, foods, places, or seasons. Those come from the Nation, through its people, on its terms.
- **Tribes are governments.** Work with a Nation is government-to-government. Tribal citizens are rights holders, relatives, community members, and partners, never "stakeholders."
- **Treaty rights are reserved, not granted.** The Tribes kept rights they already held [verify].
- **Names matter.** Use each Nation's own name for itself.
- **Plants, animals, and waters are relatives, not resources.**
- **What is shared in the room stays in the room** unless the person who shared it says otherwise. Do not record guests without permission.
- **Do your homework before you ask.** Tribal staff are busy.

## Consent levels

Each local lesson slot on these pages names the consent it needs before a site fills it.

| Level | What it means | Example |
|---|---|---|
| C0 | No consent needed: public facts about public things, cited | A public weather station, a county hazard plan, a federal program page |
| C1 | A courtesy notice to the Nation and the TCU before use | Naming the InterTribal organizations a Nation publicly works through, from their own pages |
| C2 | The Nation's confirmation before any place-specific use | A projection for a reservation's lands; ceded lands or usual and accustomed areas |
| C3 | Written permission, with its scope, for the Nation's Knowledge | Anything a knowledge holder shares; any cultural relationship to a place, species, food, or season |

Permission is specific, recorded, reviewable, and can be taken back. A private letter or email never goes onto a public page.

## No one speaks for a Nation (the D22 rule)

No learner, instructor, or page speaks or acts as a Nation, a Tribal council, an Elder, or a knowledge holder. In plain words:

- Learners work as analysts, planners, staff, and reviewers. Those roles are fine.
- Quoting a Nation's own public words, with the speaker, document, and date, is fine.
- Role plays never put a learner in a Nation's, council's, or Elder's seat. Where a source lesson had such a role play, these courses use a neutral form: staff of a county or utility, analysts mapping public positions, a review panel that speaks as itself.
- Guests and partners agree or decline as themselves.
- Classroom letters, briefs, and resolutions say "CLASSROOM EXERCISE" and are never any Nation's or organization's position.

## Data sovereignty and data care

Data about Tribal Peoples, lands, waters, and relatives belongs under the authority of the Nations it comes from. Data literacy is sovereignty; so is data care.

- **CARE Principles for Indigenous Data Governance** (Global Indigenous Data Alliance): Collective benefit, Authority to control, Responsibility, and Ethics [verify]. These are broad principles for Indigenous data everywhere.
- **OCAP®** (First Nations Information Governance Centre): Ownership, Control, Access, and Possession of First Nations data by First Nations [verify]. OCAP is a First Nations framework from Canada; it informs, but is not a rule for, U.S. Tribal Nations.
- **Local Contexts** Traditional Knowledge (TK) and Biocultural (BC) Labels (https://localcontexts.org/) are created and applied by Indigenous communities. These pages never apply them; respect any label you see.
- **Tiered Sovereign Data Framework (TSDF):** ATNI's four-tier way of putting CARE and OCAP into practice, using a watershed:

| Tier | Name | Meaning | In these courses |
|---|---|---|---|
| T0 | Rain | Open: released for public benefit by the Nation's own decision | Public data (stations, gauges, published plans) and what a Nation has chosen to publish |
| T1 | Lake | Network: shared among Indigenous network members through reciprocal protocols | Only with the Nation's direction |
| T2 | River | Negotiated: shared with outside partners through formal agreements | Not used without a formal agreement |
| T3 | Canoe | Sovereign: culturally sensitive, under the community's complete control | Stays with the student and the knowledge holder; never in papers, slides, or shared drives |

- **When unsure, treat it as T3.** Over-protecting can be corrected; under-protecting may cause harm that cannot be undone.
- **The Nation decides the tier.** Not the student, the instructor, or a tool.
- **Public data about a Nation's lands is not the Nation's voice.** A projection or screening profile for a reservation is labeled as public data and is shared beyond class only after the Nation reviews it.
- **No Tribal knowledge in public tools.** Do not post, publish, or upload Tribal knowledge, internal documents, or anything above T0, including to AI tools.
- **No exact locations** of culturally sensitive sites in any assignment.
- **Data returns to the Nation.** Anything produced with a Nation's permission is offered back to it.
- **Student work stays with students.** These pages collect no names, grades, uploads, or tracking.

## Data posture: LIVE, SNAPSHOT, TEACHING MODEL

Every data view carries one visible label and a caption with its source, issuer, and capture date.

| Label | Meaning |
|---|---|
| LIVE | A live public site or feed. The instructor opens it outside class; it is never embedded or run in class. |
| SNAPSHOT | A dated capture saved with the site's files. The only real data a page shows. |
| TEACHING MODEL | Invented or simplified numbers that show a pattern. The view says so. |

**Missing is not zero.** A blank is "unknown," with how one would find out. A total that includes a blank reads "at least." A projection always says "projection, not a prediction" and names its scenario, period, and baseline.

## Boundaries are representations, not jurisdiction

Any boundary on these pages (Census, federal, county, or a data product) is a public representation and says where it came from. It is not a statement of jurisdiction. Treaty, ceded, and usual and accustomed areas are described only as the Nation describes them.

## Hand first, then tools

Every lab starts by hand, on paper, with no internet. Then a tool may check or speed the work. Tools change, lose funding, or go offline, so the method must work without them. Tools support and never replace Tribal decision-making. Some tools cover only part of the country; each lab says "where it covers your region," and where a tool does not reach, the hand version is the whole lab.

## How the marks on these pages work

Some boxes on these pages are left open on purpose. They are invitations, not mistakes.

- `[NEEDS TRIBAL CONFIRMATION: <Nation>]`: only that Nation can supply or approve this. When no public source names the Nation, the mark reads "the Nation(s) whose homelands the campus is on." Only the Nation's recorded confirmation replaces it.
- `[LOCAL EXPERT: TCU faculty or knowledge holder]`: room for teaching that belongs to local experts and knowledge holders. Only the TCU, through its own protocols, fills it.
- `[PLACEHOLDER: ...]`: the institution or instructor supplies it (a course number, a date, a policy).
- `[INSTRUCTOR SELECTS]`: a reading slot; the instructor chooses the source.
- `[PERSONAL VOICE: Patrick writes this]`: the module author's own words, still to come.
- `[verify]`: a detail to confirm before teaching. Programs, rules, courts, and dates changed often in 2025 and 2026; check each one the term you teach it.

A mark is never replaced by a guess. Only the Nation or the TCU replaces a mark, each with its own words.

Say it plain: We ask first, we listen, we do not speak for anyone, and each Nation decides what its own knowledge is for.
